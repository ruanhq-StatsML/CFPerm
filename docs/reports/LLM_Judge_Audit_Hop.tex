\begin{abstract}
The LLM \textbf{audit / moderation / preference-judge} stream is the alignment counterpart of hallucination regime detection.
Here \(Y\) is the reviewer decision (pass/reject, or chosen/rejected), not a factuality bit.
Last-two OnlineRFPerm asks whether the \textbf{audit map} hopped between adjacent windows.
A slowly stricter policy can remain smooth.
A overnight judge swap, policy-pack cut, or annotator-pool replacement is the changepoint---``the reviewer died suddenly'', not ``the model forgot how to write.''
We post the HH-RLHF~\citep{bai2022hh-rlhf} RFPerm-as-Judge numbers already shipped: quiet \(t=2\) silent, cut \(t=4\) fires with delay 0, judge error \(0.147\to 0.539\).
\end{abstract}

\section{What is being tested}
Each batch is \((X_t,Y_t)\): content to review, and the current auditor's label.
The previous-window probe \(\mu_0\) \emph{is} yesterday's audit logic.
The hop gate is consecutive OOS, \(\gamma=1.25\):
\[
\mathrm{fire}_t=\mathbf{1}\!\{e_{\mathrm{now}}/e_{\mathrm{prev}}\ge\gamma\}.
\]
High reject rate is not a hop. A stable-strict auditor is still smooth.
The peculiar jump is when adjacent maps stop looking like one another.

\section{Two layers}
\textbf{Smoothness} (last-two): did the rubric break between \(t-1\) and \(t\)?
\textbf{Trustworthiness} (frozen launch probe): even a silky week-by-week tightening can be rejected versus the original gold before the auditor is unusable.
Keeping only last-two would miss the boiling-frog. Keeping only the launch probe would call every slow tightening a crash.
The robust serving rule uses both.

\section{What fire is allowed to mean}
On fire: the current judge is not gold; open a human contrast window on Top-\(k\) \(\texttt{po\_risk0}\); cap or refuse DPO/RLHF merges from that window.
On quiet: standard path, \(w=1\).
\(\texttt{po\_risk0}\) is residual strength versus the previous probe---not a violation score, not a moral verdict, not unique causal attribution.
