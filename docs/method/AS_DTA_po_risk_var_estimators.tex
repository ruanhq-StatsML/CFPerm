\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Unit PO-risk $u=(\varphi-\hat\tau)^2$ is heavy-tailed; $\mathrm{Var}(u)$ is
fourth-moment driven. Default estimator:
\textbf{Causal Forest leaf-size-debiased tree-to-tree variance + leaf-wise
merging}. BCF posterior when a Bayesian pipeline exists; bootstrap only as
fallback. Tabular benign/malignant board: all estimators separate
(hit-rate $8/8$); leaf-size debias removes the upward bias on benign that
would otherwise masquerade as malignant.
\end{abstract}

\section{Why a variance estimator for PO-risk}

From the Mag$\times$Var delivery notes, $\mathrm{Var}(u)$ is the critical
statistical quantity for separating benign from malignant shifts, where
\[
u_i = (\varphi_i - \hat\tau(X_i))^2,\qquad
r_i=\varphi_i-\hat\tau(X_i),\qquad
\mathrm{Var}(u)=m_4-m_2^2.
\]
$u_i$ is squared, right-skewed, heavy-tailed: its variance is driven by
$m_4$, unstable in finite samples. An adequate estimator must be
(i)~efficient with valid uncertainty, (ii)~robust to heavy tails in finite
samples, (iii)~integrated with the causal pipeline that produced
$\hat\tau$ (BCF or Causal Forest).

\textit{Key insight:} do not estimate $\mathrm{Var}(u)$ as a separate
post-hoc residual moment alone --- obtain it from the uncertainty
machinery already in the forest / Bayesian model, which regularizes
$\hat\tau$ and prevents a handful of extreme residuals from dominating
$m_4$ through $u=r^2$. Equally, $\mathrm{Var}(\hat\tau)$ itself must be
\textbf{debiased for leaf-size heterogeneity}; otherwise $\mathrm{Var}(u)$
inherits an upward bias that can masquerade as a malignant shift.

\section{Bayesian Causal Forest --- posterior variance}

In BCF, MCMC returns draws $\hat\tau^{(s)}(X_i)$. For each draw
\[
u_i^{(s)}=\bigl(\varphi_i-\hat\tau^{(s)}(X_i)\bigr)^2,
\]
\[
\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(u_i)_{\mathrm{posterior}}
=\frac1{S-1}\sum_{s=1}^S\bigl(u_i^{(s)}-\bar u_i\bigr)^2.
\]
Uncertainty propagates from the posterior of $\hat\tau$; the prior
regularizes tails; no extra refits. Best when a Bayesian pipeline is
already in use and $n\sim 10^3$--$10^4$. Limitation: MCMC speed.

\textit{Implementation status:} \texttt{rpy2} is ABI-broken against the
environment R; R packages \texttt{bcf}/\texttt{grf} are not installed.
The suite uses a \textbf{Python bagged-forest posterior} with the same
pathway (draws of $\hat\tau\to u^{(s)}\to\mathrm{Var}(u)$), and keeps
rpy2 / Rscript bridges for when \texttt{bcf} is available.

\section{Causal Forest --- leaf-size-debiased variance + leaf merge}

Naive tree-to-tree variance
\[
\widehat V_{\mathrm{naive}}
=\frac1{B(B-1)}\sum_{b=1}^B\bigl(\hat\tau_b(X_i)-\bar\tau_i\bigr)^2
\]
is \emph{biased upward}: leaf sizes $n_{b,i}$ vary, and
$\mathrm{Var}(\hat\tau_b\mid X_i)\approx\sigma^2_{\ell_b}/n_{b,i}$.
\[
\mathbb{E}[\widehat V_{\mathrm{naive}}]
=\underbrace{\tfrac1{B^2}\sum_b\tfrac{\sigma^2}{n_{b,i}}}_{\mathrm{Var}(\hat\tau)}
+\underbrace{\tfrac{B-1}{B^2}\sum_b\tfrac{\sigma^2}{n_{b,i}}}_{\text{leaf-size bias}}.
\]
Debiased estimator:
\[
\boxed{\;
\widehat V_{\mathrm{debiased}}
=\widehat V_{\mathrm{naive}}-\widehat B_{\mathrm{leaf}},
\quad
\widehat B_{\mathrm{leaf}}
=\frac{B-1}{B^2}\sum_{b=1}^B\frac{\hat\sigma^2_{b,i}}{n_{b,i}}.
\;}
\]
$O(nB)$ after leaf precompute. Map to $\mathrm{Var}(u)$ by
(i)~tree-wise $u_i^{(b)}=(\varphi_i-\hat\tau_b)^2$ (optionally
leaf-merged) then sample variance across $b$, and/or
(ii)~delta method
$\mathrm{Var}(u_i)\approx 4\,\mathrm{Var}(\hat\tau(X_i))\,(\varphi_i-\tau)^2$.

\textit{econml wiring:} \texttt{CausalForestDML.model\_final\_} $\to$
\texttt{CausalForest}; \texttt{forest.apply(X)} gives leaf indices;
\texttt{tree.tree\_.n\_node\_samples} / \texttt{impurity} supply
$n_{b,\ell}$ and $\hat\sigma^2_{b,\ell}$;
\texttt{children\_left/right} build the parent map for leaf-wise merging.
No off-the-shelf package does this merge for \texttt{CausalForestDML} ---
a small helper is the reliable route.

\section{Bootstrap --- fallback, not first choice}

Resample, refit, $u_i^{(b)}=(\varphi_i-\hat\tau^{(b)})^2$, variance across
$b$. Expensive ($B$ refits), unstable for $m_4$, no regularization of
$\hat\tau$. Use only as fallback / sanity check; if used, $B\le 500$ and
Winsorize $u$ at a high percentile.

\section{Effect on benign vs malignant (tabular)}

Same PermuCATE $\varphi$; LIVE benign (uniform shift, $Y$ intact) vs
malignant (subset attack + $Y$-break). Estimators that separate:
\textbf{8/8}. Leaf-size debias drives benign $\widehat V_{\mathrm{debiased}}$
to $\approx 0$ while malignant stays large --- the bias that would have
masqueraded as malignant is removed.
