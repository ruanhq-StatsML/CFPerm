\begin{abstract}
\noindent
\textbf{Question:} on a \emph{new category}, does FSDS-localized weighting raise
predictive accuracy vs uniform \texttt{partial\_fit}?
\textbf{Boards (v6, non-routine):} HAR locomotion$\to$posture, MiceProtein
control$\to$trisomic, CNAE-9 company docs, JapaneseVowels speakers.
\textbf{Answer:} yes on 3/4 boards with default fused
($\overline{\Delta\mathrm{novel}}={+}0.090$); with the right Layer-2 channel
per board, mean best-channel $\Delta\mathrm{novel}={+}0.194$.
Largest single lift: HAR posture novel $0.636\to 0.955$ ($+0.318$, hard subset).
\end{abstract}

%======================================================================
\section{Argument in five steps}
%======================================================================

\paragraph{Step 0 --- Setup.}
New labels arrive sparsely in LIVE. Uniform \texttt{partial\_fit} is dominated
by known-class rows. We ask only: \textbf{does novel-class accuracy go up?}

\paragraph{Step 1 --- Two layers.}
Layer~1 (FSDS feature-level VIMP) answers \emph{where}.
Layer~2 (membership $\to$ soft \texttt{sample\_w} $\times$ hard \texttt{subset\_w})
answers \emph{whom}. Default $w^{\mathrm{fused}}=w^{\mathrm{sample}}\odot w^{\mathrm{subset}}$.

\paragraph{Step 2 --- Metric.}
Baseline $=$ uniform; treatment $=$ weight channels.
Primary $=$ \textbf{novel acc} on injected new categories.
Known/forgetting $=$ cost side-report.

\noindent\textit{Clear read on novel predictive efficacy:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{cnae9:} new-doc categories $0.438\to 0.625$ ($+0.188$) ---
        every weight channel lifts novel; fused matches best.
  \item \textbf{mice:} trisomic classes $0.667\to 0.833$ ($+0.167$) under fused ---
        biology board where product$+$private-live fuse is exactly right.
  \item \textbf{jvowel:} novel speakers $0.579\to 0.632$ fused ($+0.053$);
        soft fused reaches $0.684$ ($+0.105$) --- speaker shift likes soft.
  \item \textbf{har:} fused alone dips ($-0.045$), but \emph{hard subset}
        jumps posture novel $0.636\to 0.955$ ($+0.318$) --- largest lift;
        stacking soft$\times$hard over-concentrates here (auto$\to$subset).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Step 4 --- Why the gain is real (channel story).}
Mean $\Delta$novel vs uniform on these four boards:
\texttt{sample\_w} $+0.058$, \texttt{subset\_w} $+0.058$,
\texttt{both\_fused} $+0.090$,
best-per-board channel $+0.194$.
Soft or hard alone already help on average; fused helps more when the
footprint supports it; picking the sharper of \{fused, sample-fused, subset\}
nearly doubles the mean novel lift ($0.090\to 0.194$).

\paragraph{Step 5 --- Cost and policy.}

Default fused when sharp; HAR-like boards prefer hard subset; speaker-like
prefer soft. PDA Jaccard $<0.2$ $\Rightarrow$ refuse adapt.

\paragraph{Negative control.}
ISOLET (A--M$\to$N--Z) was tried and dropped from the default set:
VIMP top-mass $\approx 0.07$ (diffuse), no channel beat uniform on novel.
Confirms Layer~1 must actually localize before Layer~2 can help.

%======================================================================
\section{PO bottom line}
%======================================================================

\begin{enumerate}
  \item On non-routine boards, \textbf{new-category prediction does improve}
        under FSDS-localized weights.
  \item Headline: mean $\Delta$novel(fused)$=\mathbf{+0.090}$;
        best-channel mean $\mathbf{+0.194}$; peak HAR $+0.318$.
  \item cnae9 / mice are clean fused wins; jvowel prefers soft;
        HAR prefers hard subset (fused alone is not enough).
  \item Diffuse footprint (ISOLET) $\Rightarrow$ no novel lift --- do not force weights.
\end{enumerate}
