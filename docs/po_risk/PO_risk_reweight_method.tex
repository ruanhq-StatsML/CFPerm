\section{Gated PO-risk reweighting}
\label{sec:po-risk-reweight}

We adapt an online predictor on a consecutive-batch stream
$B_0,B_1,\ldots$ by \emph{reweighting} the last two batches with an
instance PO-risk score. This is not AGOD (no modality attribution, no
distillation routing) and not density-ratio estimation. The default is
uniform on the last two batches. $\sqrt{\mathrm{PO}}$ weights fire only
when a consecutive out-of-sample probe jump says $P(Y\mid X)$ moved,
then anneal back.

\paragraph{Setup.}
At hop $t$ the learner has seen $B_0,\ldots,B_t$ and is scored on the
\emph{next} batch $B_{t+1}$. The train set is always the last two
batches $B_{t-1}\cup B_t$ (same rows whether the gate is quiet or on
fire). Write $T_i=0$ for rows in $B_{t-1}$ (上一批) and $T_i=1$ for
rows in $B_t$ (这一批). The next-step model is RF / XGBoost (not Ridge).

\paragraph{Probe.}
The PO probe is a shallow random forest ($n_{\mathrm{est}}=20$,
$\mathrm{depth}=4$) fit on 上一批 as $T=0$:
\begin{equation}
  \mu_0 \;=\; \mathrm{RF}(B_{t-1}).
\end{equation}
This probe is \emph{not} the next-step predictor. Consecutive OOS
errors (no $K$-fold; an in-sample $e_1>e_0$ on trees fires every hop)
\begin{equation}
  e_{\mathrm{now}}
    = \mathrm{err}\bigl(\mu_0 \text{ fit } B_{t-1}\to B_t\bigr),\qquad
  e_{\mathrm{prev}}
    = \mathrm{err}\bigl(\mu_0 \text{ fit } B_{t-2}\to B_{t-1}\bigr).
\end{equation}
The hop fires iff $e_{\mathrm{now}}/e_{\mathrm{prev}}\ge\gamma$
(default $\gamma=1.5$). The first hop is always quiet: there is no
previous OOS.

\paragraph{Instance PO-risk.}
Every observation on the last two batches gets its own score against
the $T=0$ probe. For regression,
\begin{equation}
  r_i \;=\; \bigl|Y_i - \mu_0(X_i)\bigr|.
\end{equation}
For classification, $r_i=1-p_{\mu_0}(Y_i\mid X_i)$. Mix with the batch
gap $\delta=\max(e_{\mathrm{now}}-e_{\mathrm{prev}},0)$ at mix $0.5$:
\begin{equation}
  \texttt{po\_risk0}_i
    \;=\; r_i\,\bigl(1 + 0.5\,\delta\bigr).
\end{equation}
This is a \emph{per-row} map, not a batch scalar.

\paragraph{Why PO-risk is significant only when OOD is large.}
Let $\mu_t^\star(x)=\mathbb{E}_t[Y\mid X=x]$ be the new regression and
$\mu_0$ the probe fit on 上一批. Under squared loss the OOS risk
splits exactly into irreducible noise plus the regression gap
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}_t\bigl[(Y-\mu_0(X))^2\bigr]
    \;=\;
    \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_t\bigl[(Y-\mu_t^\star(X))^2\bigr]}_{\sigma_t^2}
    \;+\;
    \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_t\bigl[(\mu_t^\star(X)-\mu_0(X))^2\bigr]}_{\Delta_t^2}.
  \label{eq:po-excess}
\end{equation}
The MAE / $1-p$ probe we use is a robust surrogate of the same split.
Quiet consecutive batches have $\Delta_t\approx 0$, so
$e_{\mathrm{now}}/e_{\mathrm{prev}}\approx 1$ and instance
$r_i$ is just noise. A large $P(Y\mid X)$ hop makes $\Delta_t$ first
order: the gate ratio is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{e_{\mathrm{now}}}{e_{\mathrm{prev}}}
    \;\approx\;
    1 + \frac{\Delta_t^2}{\sigma^2},
\end{equation}
which crosses $\gamma$ only when the concept OOD is large relative to
noise. Pointwise, $\mathbb{E}[r_i\mid X_i]\approx \sigma+| \mu_t^\star-\mu_0|(X_i)$,
so
\begin{equation}
  \sqrt{\texttt{po\_risk0}_i}
    \;\approx\;
    \begin{cases}
      \sigma, & \Delta_t \ll \sigma \quad\text{(weights collapse to uniform after mean-1)},\\[2pt]
      \bigl|\mu_t^\star(X_i)-\mu_0(X_i)\bigr|, & \Delta_t \gg \sigma \quad\text{(weights track where the map flipped)}.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Two scales move together, both $\propto\Delta_t$. The batch gap
$\delta$ inflates every $T{=}1$ weight relative to $T{=}0$; the residual
$r_i$ ranks \emph{within} $T{=}1$. That is why PO-risk is a wash on
similar / covariate hops (fire $0.00$--$0.04$) and pays on a concept
cut (fire $0.17$; next-batch MSE $2.15\to 1.56$ on the hop after $B_4$).
Density ratio $p(x)$ cannot see~\eqref{eq:po-excess}: covariate OOD
leaves $\Delta_t=0$. A \emph{global} map flip makes
$|\mu_t^\star-\mu_0|$ nearly constant on $T{=}1$, so $\sqrt{\mathrm{PO}}$
is almost a constant tilt; dropping the old batch is then the harder,
better lever. Reweighting is the right soft lever when OOD is large
\emph{and} heterogeneous in $x$.

\paragraph{Weights.}
Quiet hops stay uniform. On fire, keep the same last-two rows;
$T=0$ stays at $1$ and $T=1$ is upweighted by $\sqrt{\mathrm{PO}}$:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde w_i
    =
    \begin{cases}
      1, & \text{quiet, or } T_i=0,\\
      \sqrt{\texttt{po\_risk0}_i}, & \text{fire and } T_i=1,
    \end{cases}
  \qquad
  w_i
    = \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\frac{\tilde w_i}{\bar{\tilde w}},\, 0.05,\, 20\right).
\end{equation}
The next-step model trains on $(X_i,Y_i,w_i)_{i\in B_{t-1}\cup B_t}$
and is scored on $B_{t+1}$.

\paragraph{Annealing.}
The ratio is a one-shot heat. After a fire, the next hop's
$e_{\mathrm{prev}}$ \emph{is} that large OOS error, so
$e_{\mathrm{now}}/e_{\mathrm{prev}}\ll\gamma$ and the stream cools
back to last-two uniform. A one-shot concept cut heats once. A
periodic clock (day/night occupancy, PM2.5 episodes) reheats on a
schedule; reweighting is built for the hop after a jump, not to ride
a cycle.

\paragraph{Two levers, not one.}
Reweighting does not drop the old batch. Dropping $B_{t-1}$ and
training on $B_t$ only is a separate hard lever (residual hop-gate).
PO-tail subset localization is off: at batch size $200$ a $q=0.3$
tail is too thin. Always-on $\sqrt{\mathrm{PO}}$ and always-on DRE
lose to last-two uniform on quiet and on real consecutive clocks ---
that is why the default is uniform.

\paragraph{What this is not.}
Not AGOD: there is no modality-specific gap and no distillation
router. Not DRE: the score uses $(X,Y)$ residual risk, not $p(x)$.
Not always-on IPTW: $\sqrt{\mathrm{PO}}$ is gated. Not in-sample
$e_1>e_0$: that never anneals on trees.
