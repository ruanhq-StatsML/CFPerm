\begin{abstract}
The contribution of this note is a \emph{data formulation}, not a new
detector. Four public QA corpora---HaluEval, SQuAD, HotpotQA, and
TruthfulQA---are cast into one tidy serving stream
\texttt{t\_idx / batch / question / answer / y}, isomorphic to the HH
preference stream already used with OnlineRFPerm.
Once the table exists, the rest is standard: freeze a shallow probe on a
reference window with $n_{\mathrm{ref}}=100$, run OnlineRFPerm as an online
regime clock on the trail, and score manuscript \texttt{first}$_k$ against the
\texttt{onlinePermOOB} bank.
Quiet batches keep knowledge in the prompt; hop batches drop context and force
invent-style decoding.
Faith uses answer-precision $|A\cap K|/|A|$ (not Jaccard), so short endpoint
answers against long ambient $K$ are not diluted.
Binary $y\in\{0,1\}$ is thresholded from that continuous score; on all four
datasets OnlineRFPerm fires at the hop (delay~$0$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope}
\paragraph{What this delivers.}
A production-shaped wiring:
live or simulated LLM answers $\to$ tidy dataframe $\to$ OnlineRFPerm fire
$\to$ action route (\texttt{model\_rollback} / \texttt{retrieval\_refresh} /
\texttt{audit\_topk}).
The product claim is a \emph{timestamped quality-regime hop}, which is the
prerequisite for opening a contrast window and choosing an arm.

\paragraph{What this is not.}
Not a fact checker, not a causal root-cause engine, and not an LLM training
paper. A fire opens a window and a gate; it does not assert that any single
answer is factually wrong.

\paragraph{What else is deliberately out of scope here.}
(i)~acted-vs-ignored ¥ ledgers (optional later, if a cost ledger returns);
(ii)~agent path-subset localization (FSDS/LOGO on tool traces);
(iii)~replacing RAG evaluation suites or human fact-check queues;
(iv)~unsupervised ``semantic quality'' scores without a $Y$ source.
Those are adjacent product layers; they are not required to make the monitor
claim.

\section{Formulation of data (the justify)}
\label{sec:form}
\paragraph{Why formulate at all.}
OnlineRFPerm consumes a \emph{batch-indexed labeled stream}
$(X_t,Y_t)_{t=0}^{T-1}$, not raw JSONL and not an LLM leaderboard.
Heterogeneous QA corpora only become a serving-monitor use-case after they are
rewritten into that object. The formulation below is the isomorphism that makes
HaluEval / SQuAD / HotpotQA / TruthfulQA (and a production assistant log) the
\emph{same} input as the HH tidy preference stream.

\paragraph{Atomic row.}
One row $=$ one served turn:
\begin{verbatim}
t_idx | batch | question | answer | y
\end{verbatim}
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
column & meaning & OnlineRFPerm role \\
\midrule
\texttt{t\_idx} & stream time (serving order) & index of the online clock \\
\texttt{batch} & $\lfloor t_{\mathrm{idx}}/n_{\mathrm{per}}\rfloor$
  & window $B_t$ for fit / OOS error \\
\texttt{question} & user / query text & builds $X$ (with answer) \\
\texttt{answer} & model output at $t$ & builds $X$; production log swap \\
$y$ & badness label in $\{0,1\}$ & $Y$ for the probe \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
Auxiliary columns (\texttt{dataset}, \texttt{hopped}, \texttt{rag\_hit},
\texttt{faith}, \texttt{knowledge}, \texttt{gold}, \texttt{system}) are
\emph{audit / routing} fields. They are not required for the fire rule; they
justify how $y$ was built and which arm to pull after fire.

\paragraph{Two layers, one schema.}
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
layer & path & role \\
\midrule
source QA & \texttt{*\_source.parquet}
  & \texttt{question / knowledge / gold} (static card) \\
serving stream & \texttt{*\_stream\_table.parquet}
  & generated \texttt{answer} + $y$ + batch clock \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Isomorphism with HH.}
The HH preference stream is already
\texttt{t\_idx | batch | question | answer | y | feature}.
Inference QA uses the same skeleton; only the \emph{semantics} of $y$ change
(preference $\to$ faithfulness / correction / escalation). Feature construction
($X$) may be hash, style$\oplus$hash, or embeddings---swap the matrix, keep the
table. That is the justify sentence: \emph{LLM serving enters OnlineRFPerm by
becoming an HH-shaped tidy stream}.

\paragraph{Why four corpora collapse.}
Public QA sets differ in knowledge packaging (snippet, passage, multi-paragraph,
answer bank), but every turn still has a query, a generated answer, and a
gradable quality bit. Casting them into one schema is what lets a single
protocol ($n_{\mathrm{per}}$, cut, $n_{\mathrm{ref}}$, \texttt{first}$_k$) be
reported once rather than as four ad-hoc scripts.

\paragraph{Regime construction inside the form.}
Quiet / hop is a \emph{column discipline}, not a separate file format:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{Quiet} (batch $<$ cut): faithful system; knowledge present;
\texttt{hopped}=False. Mock responses echo a short knowledge prefix
(``endpoint-style''); live models answer from the prompt.
\item \textbf{Hop} (batch $\ge$ cut): invent system; knowledge removed;
\texttt{hopped}=True. Responses are confident fabrications
(Atlantis / made-up dates), detached from $K$.
\item \textbf{Faith score} $=$ answer-precision
$|A\cap K|/|A|\in[0,1]$---\emph{not} Jaccard.
Short grounded answers against long ambient $K$ (SQuAD / Hotpot) must not be
diluted by $|A\cup K|\approx|K|$.
\item \textbf{Label} $y\in\{0,1\}$ is the binary ORF target:
$y=1$ iff faith $<$ thr (default $0.45$), with a light invent heuristic on the
hop window. Continuous faith stays in the table for audit; the probe does
\emph{not} regress on faith.
\end{itemize}
See \texttt{docs/biz/ONLINERFPERM\_RESPONSES\_AND\_Y.md} for per-dataset
response examples and the $Y$ vs faith split.

Reference length is then automatic:
$n_{\mathrm{ref}}=n_{\mathrm{per}}\times\mathrm{cut}$.
With $n_{\mathrm{per}}=20$, cut$=5$, one gets $n_{\mathrm{ref}}=100$ without
changing the schema.

\paragraph{Production swap (same form).}
Keep the parquet columns. Replace \texttt{answer} with the serving log and $y$
with the best available label source, in priority order
\texttt{human\_correction} $>$ judge score $>$ escalation $>$ synthetic
faithfulness. Do \emph{not} invent a second table shape for ``real'' traffic.

\paragraph{What the form refuses to encode.}
The tidy stream does not claim factual correctness of a single answer, does not
encode causal root cause, and does not carry ¥. Those attach \emph{after} fire
(contrast window, route, optional ledger). Formulation stops at making the
monitor applicable.

\section{Protocol}
\paragraph{Clock.}
$n_{\mathrm{per}}=20$, $\mathrm{cut}=5$ $\Rightarrow$
$n_{\mathrm{ref}}=n_{\mathrm{per}}\times\mathrm{cut}=100$.
Total $n_{\mathrm{batches}}=10$: trail length $=100$ observations / $5$ batches.

\paragraph{OnlineRFPerm (batch).}
On consecutive batches $B_{t-1},B_t$:
\begin{align*}
\mu_0 &\leftarrow \mathrm{fit\_online\_probe}(X_{t-1},Y_{t-1}),\\
e_t &= \mathrm{err}(\mu_0; X_t,Y_t),\\
\mathrm{fire}_t &\iff e_{t-1}\ge e_{\mathrm{floor}}
  \;\wedge\; e_t/e_{t-1}\ge \gamma.
\end{align*}
Reported: first fire batch, detection delay $=\mathrm{fire}-\mathrm{cut}$,
and quiet/hop mean $y$.

\paragraph{Manuscript \texttt{first}$_k$.}
Let \texttt{det} be the boolean trail (batches \emph{after} cut only). Then
\begin{verbatim}
def first_k_consecutive(det, k):
    det = np.asarray(det, dtype=bool)
    for i in range(len(det) - k + 1):
        if det[i:i+k].all():
            return i   # trail batch index; 0 = first batch after cut
    return None
\end{verbatim}
Index $0$ is the first trail batch; --- means never.
\texttt{first}$_1$ is the headline onset; \texttt{first}$_2$/$_3$ are
sustained-onset checks (consecutive trail fires).

\paragraph{Baseline bank.}
Following \texttt{onlinePermOOB\_algo.py}: freeze a probe on the reference
window, build a scalar OOB/error stream on the trail, then run BOCPD,
Page--Hinkley, ADWIN, and frouros (DDM, STEPD, HDDMA, ECDDWT). All methods are
scored with the same trail-batch \texttt{first}$_k$ rule.

\paragraph{How to read the grid.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{Onset ($k=1$).}
On all four datasets, OnlineRFPerm and the selective baselines report onset at
trail index~$0$---aligned with the injected hop.
\item \textbf{Sustained onset ($k=2,3$).}
OnlineRFPerm typically fires once at the hop then returns quiet on later trail
batches (error ratio collapses after the jump). That is expected for a
ratio-gate clock: headline delay is~$0$, but consecutive multi-batch fires are
rare. DDM / STEPD / ECDDWT stay hot across the trail ($k=2,3=0$), which is a
different operating point---more sensitive, less selective.
\item \textbf{Streaming aside.}
Setting $n_{\mathrm{per}}=1$ makes the same form observation-streaming; the
ORF ratio-gate on single-point refits is noisier and need not fire. Keep the
default batch clock for this prototype; true streaming OOB follows the frozen
probe + \texttt{onlinePermOOB} recipe.
\end{itemize}

\section{Action routing}
On fire:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item low \texttt{rag\_hit} $\to$ \texttt{retrieval\_refresh};
\item fire with adequate retrieval support $\to$
\texttt{model\_rollback} / canary + \texttt{audit\_topk} via \texttt{po\_risk0}.
\end{itemize}
Quiet $\to$ standard path; audit weights anneal toward uniform.

\section{Discussion}
\paragraph{Claim that is supported.}
Under a shared serving form, answer-precision labels, and $n_{\mathrm{ref}}=100$,
OnlineRFPerm provides a delay-$0$ hop timestamp on all four QA streams and
matches the manuscript onset of the stronger selective baselines.

\paragraph{Caveats.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item Numbers in this note use the mock generator scaffold unless otherwise
stated. Swap to transformers/vLLM for production-facing regeneration; keep the
same parquet schema and \texttt{first}$_k$ scoring.
\item Sustained-fire ($k\ge 2$) is not OnlineRFPerm's primary claim; the primary
claim is onset delay. Report both, as in the PointCloud tables.
\item DDM/STEPD/ECDDWT consecutive fires should be read as sensitivity, not as
a free win: a serving monitor that stays permanently on is operationally costly.
\item $n_{\mathrm{per}}=1$ streaming uses the same form but a different clock;
single-point ORF refits are noisier---default remains the batch clock.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What else (adjacent, not required for this claim).}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item Production log adapter + $Y$-source dictionary
(\texttt{human\_correction} $>$ judge $>$ escalation $>$ synthetic).
\item Gate / $e_{\mathrm{floor}}$ calibration on quiet false-fire rate.
\item Path-subset localization after fire (agent tool traces).
\item Optional acted-vs-ignored cost ledger once unit economics exist.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item The hard justify is the data form:
\texttt{t\_idx / batch / question / answer / y} makes LLM serving isomorphic to
the HH OnlineRFPerm stream. Detectors, gates, and routes then reuse existing
machinery.
\item With that form, answer-precision $Y$, and $n_{\mathrm{ref}}=100$, all four
datasets show delay-$0$ fires at the injected hop.
\item Baselines must be scored with the same trail-batch \texttt{first}$_k$ rule;
raw delay alone is not enough for manuscript comparison.
\item Remaining work is hardening inside the form (real generator, quiet
false-fire calibration)---not inventing a new table shape or reverting to Jaccard.
\end{enumerate}
