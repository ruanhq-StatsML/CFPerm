\begin{abstract}
The attribution is univariate.
Each graph feature is one column, scored by the same concept-drift SSE drop and the same mean shift.
A dictionary of ten million columns is therefore the same procedure as the dictionary of twenty-three columns already run.
On this machine a screen of \(2\times 10^{4}\) noise columns costs \(14.8\,\mu\mathrm{s}\) each, which extrapolates to about \(150\) seconds for \(10^{7}\) columns.
Eighty permutations on every one of those columns is a few hours, so the permutations belong on the columns the screen ranks first.
The measured searches show why the dictionary can be large: most columns stay silent, and the outcome drop sits on a few of them.
\end{abstract}

\section{The column}
An episode is one finished search.
\(Y\in\{0,1\}\) is whether that search returned a solved state.
A graph feature is any number attached to the committed path: depth, children, siblings, a centrality, a path-score moment, a state coordinate.
Write \(x\) for one such number across \(n\) episodes, and write \(\mathrm{batch}\) for the indicator of episodes after the frozen reference.
The concept contribution of that column is
\[
\Delta(x)
=
\mathrm{SSE}(Y\sim 1+\mathrm{batch})
-
\mathrm{SSE}(Y\sim 1+\mathrm{batch}+x+\mathrm{batch}\cdot x).
\]
The covariate contribution is the absolute change in the mean of \(x\) across the drift.
Neither statistic looks at any other column.
A dictionary \(x_1,\ldots,x_p\) is \(p\) repetitions of this pair.
No joint model of the ten million columns is required to rank them.

\section{What a small dictionary already did}
Two searches, forty episodes, drift at episode 16.
The dictionary had twenty-three columns in six layers.
Shares are sums of positive \(\Delta(x)\) inside the layer.

\midrule
Statistics & 0.354 & 0.000 \\
History & 0.294 & 0.732 \\
Interaction & 0.164 & 0.136 \\
State encoding, four numbers & 0.095 & 0.132 \\
Graph & 0.094 & 0.000 \\
Context & 0.000 & 0.000 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

On Game of 24, \(Y\) falls from \(1.00\) to \(0.25\).
Score entropy, the rolling mean, and score times path length each have permutation \(p=0.012\).
Sibling count, the best graph column, moves by \(0.88\) and has \(p=0.074\).
Beam width and maximum depth contribute \(\Delta=0\).
On the one-step offer search the tree shape is constant, so every graph column has \(\Delta=0\) and mean shift \(0\).
\(Y\) falls from \(0.50\) to \(0.167\).
The previous outcome takes share \(0.732\), with \(p=0.012\).
The offer-identity coordinate shifts by \(1\) and has concept \(p=0.42\): it restates the batch bit, and the interaction does not add a separate piece of \(Y\).

Those zeros are the reason to admit a large dictionary.
Columns that do not move, and columns that only rename the batch, fall out.
The ranking does not need a weight assigned to ``graph'' in advance.

\section{Cost of ten million columns}
The screen below is one least-squares fit of four coefficients per column, on \(n=40\), with no permutation.
Twenty thousand standard-normal columns took \(0.296\) seconds on this machine, \(14.8\,\mu\mathrm{s}\) per column.
At that rate, \(10^{7}\) columns take about \(150\) seconds.
The same microbenchmark with \(80\) permutations on \(50\) columns cost \(2.3\,\mathrm{ms}\) per column, which extrapolates to a few hours for every one of \(10^{7}\) columns.
The permutations were counted with two fits inside the shuffle loop, so the figure is an upper envelope rather than a tuned kernel.

The practical order is the one the statistic suggests.
Screen all \(p\) columns by \(\Delta(x)\).
Permute the top of that list, or permute until the \(p\)-values separate.
A million copies of one step function will tie with each other and with the batch; they do not become a million discoveries.
Sibling count is a discovery only if its own \(\Delta\) and its own shuffle still stand after the copies are added.

\section{What is not in the dictionary}
This run did not build \(10^{7}\) graph features, and it did not fit a 768-dimensional encoder.
The state encoding is four numbers.
The current episode's residual is not a column, because that residual contains \(Y\).
Unit prices and AUC coefficients are not inputs.
The claim that does follow from the runs is narrower:
the attribution is a sort of columns, the sort is cheap relative to opening a budget on a tied column, and a fixed tree contributes a block of exact zeros that a larger graph dictionary is free to contain.
