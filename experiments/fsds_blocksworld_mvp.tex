This is the same column-wise procedure on a second search.
Four blocks, beam 4, depth 4, forty episodes, drift at episode 16, judge mix \(0.30\).
\(Y=1\) when the goal tower \((A,B,C,D)\) is returned.
The state encoding is four numbers in \([0,1]\): stack count, tallest stack, goal-prefix length, and whether \(D\) is clear.
No text encoder, no preset layer weights, no AUC.

\(Y\) moves from \(1.00\) to \(0.542\).

On Game of 24 the graph layer was \(9\%\) of the SSE and the score profile was the larger part.
Here the committed tower encoding is \(56\%\) and the child count is \(29\%\), and both clear \(p=0.012\).
The drifted judge is expanding a different frontier: more or fewer legal moves, and a different tower shape.
History does not clear \(0.10\). Beam and maximum depth stay fixed, so context stays near zero.

The screen is unchanged.
One column, one SSE drop, one mean shift.
Blocksworld only changes which columns are allowed to be nonzero.
